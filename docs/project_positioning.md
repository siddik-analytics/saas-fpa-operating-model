## 7. Interview introduction

> I'll describe a project rather than a job, because it shows the reasoning better.
>
> I built a full FP&A stack for a synthetic SaaS company around one Board reforecast and one
> decision: should they hire more sales reps, given a 24-month runway policy? The interesting part
> was that the answer came out "no", and not for the obvious reason. Runway allowed the hire. But
> modelling sales capacity and pipeline as separate constraints showed capacity was already in
> surplus — most forecast months were demand-bound — and the hiring case returned about a dollar of
> ARR for every four of cash.
>
> So the recommendation was to relieve pipeline, not add headcount, and to flag that the downside
> scenario breached the runway floor. I built it so those two questions — can we afford it, and is
> it worth it — get answered separately, because a runway test on its own will approve a bad
> investment.

## Positioning rules

**Always:** independent portfolio case study · synthetic company and synthetic data · state it
once, clearly, then let the work speak.

**Never:** imply Helio is a real employer, that the figures are real company financials, that this
was professional employment, that these were actual Board materials, or that any real customer or
company information was used.

**Lead with the finance.** The stack is credible, but the differentiator is the judgement: a
recommendation that contradicts the stakeholder, affordability and attractiveness separated, and
limitations stated without prompting.
